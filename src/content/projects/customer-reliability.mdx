## Overview

This notebook investigates how observed repayment arrears vary with the number of children, marital status, income category and loan purpose. It follows a structured process of reviewing, cleaning, analysing and summarising a customer dataset.

The bank credit department is the notebook’s case-study scenario; the project is presented here as an analytical study.

## The Challenge

The source table contains missing values, inconsistent text, duplicate records and implausible values. These issues need to be addressed before customer groups can be compared meaningfully.

## Approach & Development

Using pandas, the notebook inspects the dataset and applies a documented cleaning workflow:

- Fill missing income and employment-duration values using employment-type group medians.
- Convert negative employment durations to positive values and remove anomalous child counts.
- Standardise text, handle duplicates and adjust data types.
- Group income into bands and normalise loan purposes into four categories.
- Build pivot tables with counts and proportions of customers with repayment arrears.

## Key Challenges & Solutions

Group sizes differ substantially, so the analysis compares proportions alongside counts. The notebook also identifies remaining anomalies in employment duration and addresses zero ages as missing data.

Some categories contain very few observations. Their percentages should be treated cautiously, and the cleaning assumptions remain part of the interpretation.

## Final Result

The output is a set of reproducible comparisons answering the four research questions. For loan purpose, the notebook reports:

| Loan purpose | Records with arrears | Total records | Observed share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Car | 400 | 4,279 | 9.3% |
| Education | 369 | 3,988 | 9.3% |
| Wedding | 183 | 2,305 | 7.9% |
| Real estate | 739 | 10,120 | 7.3% |

<Callout>
These are descriptive associations in the supplied dataset. They do not establish causes, individual creditworthiness, or a validated lending model. The portfolio chart is redrawn from the notebook’s reported values.
